how it works

Three moves, and you know what comes next.

Nothing to set up before you start: the button in the middle of the wheel is already the file picker.

Hand it a track

Tap the button at the centre of the wheel and pick a file from the device — mp3, m4a, wav or flac. The whole track is read, in a few seconds, while a ring counts the work down and the wheel waits.

Read the key it found

The wheel lights back up on the sector it landed on, ringed in red. The panel underneath carries the Camelot code and the chord name, and the key goes into the file’s own tags: open the track again, here or in another program, and it is already there.

See what mixes with it

Pick one of the six rules and the wheel keeps lit only the keys that rule allows, dimming everything else. Close the lock and a key that does not mix cannot even be chosen.

what is inside

Everything a set needs. Nothing it doesn’t.

No library to import, no cloud to sync, no account to make. It opens on the wheel and it stays there.

Twenty-four sectors, a colour each

Minors on the inner ring, majors on the outer one, and a fixed colour per key. After a week you stop reading the labels and go by the colour.

Camelot codes or chord names

Read the wheel as 12A and 1B, or as C♯m and G the way they sit on the circle of fifths. One switch at the top, and the app comes back the way you left it.

Six rules for the mix

From every compatible key down to a single move. The rule you picked survives closing the app, because it is the way you work, not a setting you happened to touch.

A lock on the rules

Closed, the wheel only lets you move to a key that mixes. Open, you go wherever you want. It is the difference between practising and improvising.

The whole track, in seconds

Every analysis listens to the whole track, with harmonium, the key engine written from scratch for this app. Every key remembers which version found it, so a track is analysed again only when the answer could change.

The track’s tags, yours to fix

Title, artists, album, genre, year and dozens more come out of the file, and go back into it when you correct them, on mp3 and wav. Choose which ones the track card shows; when the tags are missing, the file name stands in.

Listen while you choose

The key badge is the play button. The track starts and stops there, without a second screen and without leaving the wheel.

The waveform, under your finger

The track’s waveform scrolls as it plays, and the part already played takes the colour. Drag it to move, or jump ten seconds at a time, to the start, or to the end.

Five languages, two themes

English, Italian, French, Spanish and German, light and dark. Both follow the phone until you decide otherwise.

mixing rules

Six rules, from the safest to the boldest.

A rule is a set of moves around the wheel. Choose one and the wheel becomes the answer to a single question: from here, where can I go?

01

All compatible keys

The full set. Everything that will sit next to the key you are on, without ranking it.

02

Without cross tone transition

The same set minus the jump across the rings. What is left is the ground nobody argues about.

03

Adjacent keys

Only the neighbours: one step round the wheel, or straight across to the other ring. The move you can make blind.

04

Energy boost/lower

Two steps up or two down: the moves that lift a floor, and the ones that bring it back to earth.

05

Semitone higher

One move and one only, seven sectors away — a semitone up. The oldest lift there is.

06

Compatible tone

Three sectors back with a change of ring. Further from home than it looks, and still consonant.

Reading a Camelot code

The number is the position on the circle of fifths, from 1 to 12; the letter is the ring — A for minor, B for major. Two tracks mix when their codes are the same, or one apart, or share the number and swap the letter. That is the whole notation, and the wheel does the arithmetic for you.

phone & tablet

Before you press play

The analysis happens on the phone. The file you pick is read where it sits, and neither it nor the result ever leaves the device — there is no server of ours in the middle, which is also why there is no account. Four formats are read: mp3, m4a, wav and flac. Anything else is turned down rather than guessed at.
